The workflow

Merge to measured in four steps

One rail from pull request to full traffic. Nothing ships without a plan, nothing rolls back without a receipt.

1

Connect

Install the GitHub or GitLab app. {{BRAND}} maps services from your repo topology in under a minute.

2

Gate

Every merge lands behind a flag. Choose canary cohorts by region, plan tier, or a 1% random slice.

3

Watch

Error rate, p95 latency, and your own Datadog monitors decide promotion. Humans approve, robots enforce.

4

Promote

Traffic steps 1% to 25% to 100% on your curve. Any breach reverts in under 30 seconds, then pings Slack.

Auto-rollback

Bad deploys revert themselves

Set a threshold once: error rate over 2%, p95 over 400ms, or any Datadog or Grafana alert. When a canary breaches it, {{BRAND}} freezes promotion, restores the last good build, and posts the diff, the metric, and the timeline to your incident channel. On-call reads a receipt, not a mystery.

FAQ

Questions engineers actually ask

Does {{BRAND}} replace our CI, or sit after it?

After it. Your pipeline builds and tests as usual; {{BRAND}} takes the artifact from there and owns rollout, gating, and rollback. GitHub Actions, CircleCI, and Buildkite all work with a one-line step.

What happens during a rollback, exactly?

We repoint traffic to the last build that passed your thresholds, freeze all in-flight promotions for that service, and post the metric breach, the diff, and the timeline link to Slack. Median revert time across customers is 22 seconds.

Do you support Kubernetes and serverless together?

Yes. The same rollout plan can span a Kubernetes deployment, a Vercel project, and a Lambda alias. Traffic percentages stay in lockstep across all three.

Can we self-host?

Business customers can run the control plane in their own VPC via Terraform module. Data plane agents are always yours; we never proxy production traffic.
